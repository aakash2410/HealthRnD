Janitri / DotoCare / Sybil / FetalLite
AI CTG interpretation: automated fetal distress classification (normal / suspicious / pathological). Sensors: abdominal (tocotransducer + ultrasound probe for FHR), arm (automated BP cuff), chest (SpO₂/RR). FetalLite: portable wireless fetal monitor for lower-resource labor wards.
→ FHIR Observation (CTG classification) · real-time alert to obstetrician
Gradi / Dozee / Stryker–Titan
Quantified blood loss measurement (Gradi drape) replaces visual estimation; Dozee contactless vitals monitoring detects early PPH deterioration pattern; Stryker–Titan surgical fluid management system for intraoperative PPH control in C-section / operative delivery.
→ FHIR Observation (blood loss quantified) · AI early-warning alert to team · intraoperative fluid balance to HMIS
Intrapartum AI Ultrasound (fetal presentation + EFW)
Standard ultrasonography + AI assistance at admission: confirms fetal lie and presentation, estimates fetal weight (EFW) before operative delivery, and identifies cord presentation. Reduces clinical examination error before CS decision.
→ FHIR DiagnosticReport (presentation, EFW) · confirms or modifies delivery plan
HIE-CM: ANC record pulled at admission
Labor team pulls mother's ANC risk record (gestational age, pre-eclampsia risk, parity, prior complications) from ABHA via HIE-CM consent — no starting from scratch
→ Full ANC context available in labor room within seconds of admission
Immediate
Postpartum 0–24 hours
Current state
  • Maternal monitoring: manual vitals at intervals — heavy workload means observations missed in the 2-hour danger window post-delivery
  • PPH identification relies on periodic nursing checks; delayed escalation common
  • Newborn: APGAR assessed subjectively; birth weight on beam balance (inaccurate); length rarely measured correctly

  • Discharge summary: paper or basic HMIS printout; mother has no digital copy
  • No standard handoff to community ASHA for postnatal follow-up

Care 0–28 days
Current state
  • NICU monitoring: intermittent nursing observations; respiratory distress identified by visible signs — intervention delayed
  • Neonatal sepsis: detected late from clinical signs (poor feeding, lethargy, temperature) — high case fatality at this stage

AI-enabled state
InAccel Saans · Vayu CPAP · Draeger/Syrma Johari BabyFlow 100
Three Gates-supported CPAP solutions covering the full spectrum: InAccel Saans: FPGA AI for continuous SpO₂/HR/RR monitoring and CPAP titration (1 nurse per 40–50 babies); Vayu: portable bubble CPAP for low-resource NICUs, minimal consumables; BabyFlow 100 (Draeger/Syrma Johari): high-flow nasal CPAP for district hospitals without CPAP expertise.
→ FHIR Observation (respiratory status, CPAP parameters) → Child ABHA · NHCX NICU claim trigger
IoT-Enabled Incubator (smart, connected)
Connected incubators (e.g., Draeger Caleo, Comen) with continuous temperature, humidity, SpO₂, and weight monitoring. Bluetooth/Wi-Fi push to HMIS; automated alarms for temperature drift and apnea events; daily weight logged without manual charting.
→ FHIR Observation (temp, humidity, weight) · alarm log to nursing station · weight trend in Child ABHA
Neonatal Sepsis AI — Dozee (Gates-named)
Dozee contactless monitoring captures continuous HR variability, temperature instability, and RR changes; AI pattern detection flags early sepsis hours before clinical signs (poor feeding, lethargy) appear — triggers antibiotic review at the cot-side
→ FHIR Observation (sepsis risk score) · alert to neonatologist
Shishu Maapan + ARMMAN post-discharge
Serial growth tracking (day 7, 14, 28) via ASHA smartphone; WHO z-scores auto-computed; ARMMAN IVR schedules postnatal danger-sign reminders and vaccine due-dates to mother
→ Growth trend in Child ABHA · Poshan Tracker fed automatically · U-WIN vaccine reminders
Complete 28-day longitudinal record via ABHA
By day 28: one Child ABHA-linked record contains birth anthropometry, NICU events, growth trajectory, vaccinations, and community follow-up — accessible to any authorized provider via HIE-CM consent
→ NICU + community + programme data linked in one place for the first time

2. Edge & Digitisation
Siloed, manual digitisation
· HMIS entry by data entry operator — delayed, error-prone
· No standard data format — each system uses its own schema
· ANC records in facility HMIS; labour notes in paper register; NICU in separate logbook
· Triple re-entry into RCH portal, U-WIN, Poshan Tracker per visit
→
FHIR-native, single-capture
· Device gateway normalises all payloads → FHIR R4 Observation (LOINC coded) at the edge
· HMIS acts as HIP: assembles ANC encounter, intrapartum events, birth summary as FHIR bundle
· Single capture → RCH + U-WIN + Poshan Tracker automatically via ABDM sync
· EkaCare / Eka Doc: AI/NLP converts clinical notes to structured FHIR at front desk
3. Clinical & AI
Clinical judgment only — no AI
· Risk identification: subjective — depends on individual clinician's experience
· Gestational age: Naegele's rule (inaccurate for Indian population with irregular cycles)
· No AI CTG interpretation — fetal distress identified late
· No early-warning score for maternal deterioration or sepsis
· No predictive model for ANC dropout or high-risk escalation
· Clinical decision support: absent
→
AI risk models + human-in-loop sign-off
★ ARMMAN / Garbhini-GA2: India-specific gestational age AI; dropout-risk prediction → IVR auto-scheduled
Janitri / DotoCare / Sybil / FetalLite: AI CTG classification (normal / suspicious / pathological) + maternal EWS; FetalLite for lower-resource labor wards
Gradi + Dozee + Stryker–Titan: quantified PPH (Gradi drape), contactless deterioration EWS (Dozee), intraoperative fluid/surgical PPH management (Stryker–Titan)
★ InAccel Saans + Vayu CPAP + BabyFlow 100: full CPAP ecosystem: AI-monitored NICU (Saans), portable low-resource CPAP (Vayu), high-flow district hospital CPAP (BabyFlow); IoT incubators; Dozee neonatal sepsis AI
All AI outputs: FHIR DiagnosticReport · clinician/ANM signs off before action · model + version recorded as provenance
4. ABDM Platform
No shared identity or consent layer
· No common patient ID — each system (HMIS, U-WIN, Poshan, ARMMAN) uses its own local ID
· No consent mechanism — patient has no control over who accesses her records
· No interoperability — AI outputs from individual apps cannot be shared across providers
· Records don't travel with the patient; referral means starting over
· Maternity claims: submitted separately to each insurer portal (manual, 4–7 day turnaround)
· No longitudinal record exists — no single place to see the full mother-baby journey
→
ABDM Platform — NHA Digital Public Infrastructure
ABHA / Child ABHA
Persistent ID links every AI output, device reading, and clinical note to the same mother and child across all providers and programmes
HIE-CM
Patient-controlled consent artefact · encrypted FHIR bundle transfer HIP ↔ HIU · AI risk scores travel with the patient to any authorized provider

5. Programmes & Feedback
Fragmented, no feedback loop
· RCH portal, U-WIN, Poshan Tracker: separate systems, no linkage, no shared patient ID
· ANM/ASHA: no AI-generated recall lists — manual review of registers to identify overdue visits
· Mother: no digital record, no visibility into her own care data
· After discharge: community ASHA is unaware of what happened at the facility
· At 28 days: no single longitudinal record — data scattered across 4–5 registers and apps
→
Connected, AI-routed feedback
· Single FHIR push → RCH portal + U-WIN + Poshan Tracker simultaneously — no re-entry
· ANM / ASHA: AI-generated recall lists (ARMMAN dropout scores); high-risk flag routed automatically
· Mother: EkaCare PHR delivers AI-summarised discharge note, vaccine reminders, growth alerts
· Post-discharge: ARMMAN IVR postnatal programme; Shishu Maapan growth at day 7/14/28
· At 28 days: complete ANC → intrapartum → NICU → community record linked in Child ABHA — first time this exists
