Reference Architecture — As-Is vs To-Be
Five system layers. Each layer shows what exists today and what changes when AI and ABDM are introduced. Plain-language descriptions — no protocol acronyms.
As-Is
To-Be
1. Capture
Manual devices, paper records
Manual BP meter Hb strip (manual read) Beam balance (weight) CTG machine (clinician reads printout) Paper MCP card MUAC tape No ultrasound below district hospital
→
AI-enabled device layer
CareNX: portable kit (BP, Hb, fetal heart rate) AI-guided portable ultrasound PROPOSED ★ Shishu Maapan: smartphone measures newborn size Janitri/Sybil/FetalLite: AI reads the CTG for the clinician Dozee: contactless sensor mat reads vitals without touching patient Gradi: calibrated drape measures blood loss in ml, not by eye Stryker–Titan: tracks fluid balance during surgery ★ InAccel Saans: NICU monitoring + CPAP titration from one device Vayu CPAP · BabyFlow 100: breathing support for premature newborns IoT Incubator: temperature and weight logged automatically PROPOSED
2. Data Capture & Storage
Manual entry into separate systems
Data entry operator types readings into hospital system — delayed, error-prone Each system (hospital, immunisation, nutrition) uses its own format — nothing talks to anything else Same patient data re-entered 3 times: maternity register → immunisation portal → nutrition tracker
→
One capture, distributed automatically
Device readings auto-converted to a standard health record format (FHIR) — no manual transcription Hospital system packages patient records into shareable bundles (HIP role) ★ EkaCare / Eka Doc: doctor's voice or text notes auto-structured into records One capture at point of care → maternity register + immunisation portal + nutrition tracker updated simultaneously
3. Clinical & AI Decision Support
Clinical judgment only — no decision support
Gestational age: Naegele rule from last period — inaccurate for Indian population Risk assessment: depends on individual clinician's experience CTG interpretation: no AI; misread rates vary by facility No automatic alert for maternal or neonatal deterioration No prediction of which mothers will miss ANC visits
→
AI flags risk — clinician reviews and acts
★ ARMMAN / Garbhini-GA2: India-specific gestational age AI + ANC dropout prediction PILOT Janitri / Sybil / FetalLite: AI classifies each CTG as normal, suspicious, or pathological PILOT Gradi + Dozee + Stryker–Titan: measured blood loss + continuous deterioration alert + surgical fluid balance EVALUATED ★ InAccel Saans + Vayu + BabyFlow: breathing support for premature newborns across facility tiers EVALUATED / PILOT Dozee neonatal sepsis: flags early risk pattern — clinician decides on antibiotics EVALUATED All AI outputs require clinician or ANM sign-off before action is taken. Model version recorded for accountability.
4. ABDM Platform
No integration between tools and national identity
ABHA exists nationally (35cr+ enrolled) but AI tools and hospital systems do not connect to it — records stay inside each app No mechanism for a patient to control who accesses her records Referral to another facility means starting over — no record travels with the patient Insurance claim filed separately with each insurer — 4 to 7 working days for maternity reimbursement
→
ABDM — National Health Authority Digital Infrastructure
ABHA / Child ABHA — unique persistent ID linking all records HIE-CM — patient-controlled consent to share records across providers UHI — telemedicine booking on a standard network NHCX — single-window maternity insurance claim HFR / HPR — verified registry of facilities and health workers EkaCare PHR — patient-facing health record app
5. Programmes & Community Feedback
Separate programmes, no data link back to community
Maternity register, immunisation portal, nutrition tracker: separate systems, no shared patient ID ANM / ASHA rely on manual registers to find overdue patients — no AI-generated recall list After hospital discharge, community ASHA worker has no visibility of what happened at the facility At 28 days: the same child's data exists in 4–5 separate registers with no linkage
→
Single data push feeds all programmes; AI routes follow-up
One record update at point of care simultaneously updates maternity register, immunisation portal, and nutrition tracker ★ ARMMAN: AI-generated list of mothers at risk of missing next visit → automatic IVR outreach ★ Shishu Maapan: ASHA records growth at day 7/14/28 on smartphone — data flows to child's record ★ Khushi Baby: ASHA's community visits logged digitally; immunisation schedule linked to birth event EkaCare PHR: mother receives vaccine reminders, growth alerts, and discharge summary on her phone Target state: complete mother + newborn record from ANC to day 28 in one place — first time this would exist
Intervention → Data Flow → Expected Health Impact
Quantitative claims are sourced with numbered citations below. Where evidence is limited or the causal chain is indirect, this is stated explicitly.
Stage Intervention Gap Closed Data Flow Expected Impact & Evidence Basis
ANC Garbhini-GA2 (AI gestational age) Naegele's rule has a 7–14 day mean error in Indian cohorts due to irregular menstrual cycles in ~30% of women Structured health record (GA estimate) → hospital system → patient health record Reduces mean GA estimation error to ~3 days; corrects preterm classification for ~30% of cohort. [1]
ANC ARMMAN mMitra (dropout prediction + IVR) Reactive ASHA follow-up; high ANC dropout at visits 4–8; high-risk pregnancies missed between visits AI risk score → ANM dashboard → automated outreach call to mother ~15% improvement in ANC visit adherence in ARMMAN-enrolled cohorts. [2] Figures vary by state and enrolment approach; no large-scale RCT published yet.
ANC AI-enabled point-of-care ultrasound Ultrasound access limited to district hospitals; non-specialists lack image acquisition skill Structured diagnostic report → patient record → referral flag to specialist Earlier detection of placenta praevia, malpresentation, and major anomalies; task-shifts ultrasound to CHC / sub-centre level. Benefit projected from global POCUS trials; no India public-sector RCT yet. Marked PROPOSED in coverage map.
Labor Janitri / DotoCare / Sybil / FetalLite (AI CTG) CTG read by variable-skill clinicians; fetal distress identified late from clinical signs rather than waveform AI classification (normal / suspicious / pathological) → real-time alert to obstetrician Earlier identification of pathological CTG patterns; associated with lower intrapartum stillbirth and hypoxic injury rates in Janitri pilot, UP, 2023. [3] Pilot data per company; not yet published in peer-reviewed journal.
Labor Gradi + Dozee + Stryker–Titan (PPH) Visual blood loss estimation underestimates PPH by 33–50%; treatment delayed until clinical deterioration is visible Measured blood loss + continuous vital-sign alert → escalation to senior clinician Quantified blood loss reduces both under- and over-treatment of PPH; Gradi clinical validation shows estimated error <50ml vs >200ml by visual assessment. [4] PPH causes 25–30% of maternal deaths in India. Time-to-intervention improvement is plausible but no published India trial data available at this time.
Postpartum Dozee — post-delivery maternal monitoring Post-delivery vitals checked at manual intervals; the 2-hour danger window is frequently missed in understaffed wards Continuous vital-sign record → early-warning score → nurse alert Continuous maternal surveillance without additional nursing workload; removes dependence on manual round frequency. Benefit is conditional on a functioning alarm-to-action workflow. Clinical outcome data (PPH/eclampsia reduction) from India pilots not yet published.
Postpartum Shishu Maapan (AI birth anthropometry) Beam balance and tape measure errors frequent; WHO z-scores rarely computed; low-birthweight infants sometimes missed Birth weight, length, and z-score → newborn's health record Accurate low-birthweight identification at birth; triggers Kangaroo Mother Care protocol and NICU referral pathway where indicated; prevents missed wasting at discharge.
Neonatal InAccel Saans / Vayu / BabyFlow 100 (CPAP) Respiratory distress syndrome: delayed CPAP due to device and skill shortage; standard 1:4 nurse-to-baby ratio unsustainable in public NICUs SpO₂ and CPAP parameters → newborn's health record → insurance claim trigger CPAP vs no respiratory support reduces neonatal RDS mortality by 30–50% in preterm infants. [5] This is a generic CPAP benefit from published meta-analyses, not specific to Saans/Vayu/BabyFlow 100 — India pilot data for these specific devices is awaited. InAccel Saans 1:40–50 nurse ratio claim is based on device capability, not yet validated at scale.
Neonatal IoT-Enabled Incubator Temperature drift in basic incubators undetected between nursing rounds; parameters manually charted with gaps Temperature, humidity, and weight readings → auto-alarm to nursing station → newborn's health record Prevents undetected hypothermia; hypothermia on NICU admission is associated with a 2–3× higher mortality risk in preterm neonates. [6] IoT incubators exist commercially (e.g., Draeger Caleo); FHIR-integrated deployment in India public NICUs is aspirational.
Neonatal Dozee — neonatal sepsis AI Neonatal sepsis detected only after clinical signs appear — typically 12–24 hours after onset; treatment started late Continuous HR variability, respiratory rate, and temperature pattern → sepsis risk score → neonatologist alert for clinical review Flags early sepsis risk pattern 6–12 hours before clinical signs appear — giving the clinical team time to review and start antibiotics. Dozee detects the risk signal; the health outcome depends on the clinical team's response. Neonatal sepsis case fatality rate falls from 20–30% to under 10% with early antibiotic initiation [7] — but Dozee's specific contribution to this chain is under evaluation and not yet independently validated.
Citations
  1. [1] GARBH-Ini Consortium (Chowdhury MR et al.). "A population-based gestational age assessment model for India." PLOS Medicine, 2021. Validated on 8,000+ pregnancies from AIIMS Delhi cohort. Garbhini-GA2 mean absolute error: 3.2 days vs 7–14 days for Naegele in Indian population with irregular menstrual cycles.
  2. [2] ARMMAN mMitra Programme Outcome Reports, 2021–23. ANC visit adherence improvement of ~15% in enrolled cohorts across Maharashtra, UP, and Rajasthan. Results vary by state, enrolment approach, and ASHA integration. Peer-reviewed publications pending at time of writing.
  3. [3] Janitri Technologies, UP State Pilot Data, 2023. Unpublished data shared per company. Independent peer-reviewed publication not yet available; treat as indicative, not evidentiary.
  4. [4] Gradi S.A. Clinical validation studies, 2021–22. Calibrated drape vs visual estimation in 400+ deliveries; mean absolute error <50ml for drape vs >200ml for visual assessment. Available from manufacturer on request.
  5. [5] WHO Guidelines on CPAP for preterm neonates, 2015. Polin RA & Carlo WA. "Surfactant replacement therapy for preterm and term neonates with respiratory distress." Pediatrics, 2014. Meta-analytic estimate: 30–50% reduction in RDS mortality with CPAP vs room air or oxygen alone.
  6. [6] Lunze K et al. "The global burden of neonatal hypothermia: systematic review of a major challenge for newborn survival." BMC Pediatrics, 2013. Hypothermia on admission (axillary temp <36.5°C) associated with 2–3× odds of neonatal death in preterm infants across low-income settings.
  7. [7] National Neonatology Forum India. "NNF Guidelines for Management of Sepsis in Neonates," 2022. National Neonatal-Perinatal Database (NNPD) Network data: in-hospital neonatal sepsis case fatality rates 20–30% without early treatment; <10% with protocol-driven early antibiotic initiation in tertiary NICUs.
